# Rhythm and retention — why the voice moves hold attention

The moves in `sentence-rhythm.md` are a voice. They are also, not by accident, a
retention system. A writer who applies them gets the retention benefit whether or not
they know why. This file explains the why — so you can reason about a move deliberately
when the substrate gives you a choice, instead of applying it on feel alone.

**This file adds no moves.** Every mechanism below is already in `sentence-rhythm.md` or
`example-passages.md`. Load this when you want to understand what a move is *doing* to
the viewer. Keep drafting from `sentence-rhythm.md`.

## The frame — hook and payoff are one structure

The strongest finding across professional screenwriting and YouTube retention research
is the one Step 4b already encodes: a video is a **hook and a payoff**, and the writing
is the line connecting them. One end promises something; the other end delivers it. Step
4b carves the hook *from* the body's strongest beat for exactly this reason — the hook is
not an introduction, it is the front half of a promise whose back half is the body's
strongest moment.

This is also why hook-first drafting fails (see `learnings/script-writer-spec-over-restriction.md`):
you cannot write the front of a promise before you know what the back of it is.

## Move 2, "But" pivots — causal weight between beats

"And then" links two events with zero causal weight. "But" and "therefore" carry it:
"but" signals a complication or reversal, "therefore" signals a consequence. This is the
load-bearing structural principle in professional screenwriting — replace "and then"
between beats with "but" or "therefore".

Move 2 already does half of this at the sentence hinge. The retention mechanism: a
sentence-initial "but" pauses the viewer's expectation and forces them to stay for the
completion. "And then" sentences can be left at any point; "but" sentences cannot —
"But text is tiny", "But here is where the nightmare begins" are each a small contract
that the next clause will pay off.

The deliberate extension, when the substrate supports it: the same logic applies
*between paragraphs*, not just inside sentences. When two beats are merely sequential the
prose goes slack. When the substrate genuinely has a complication or a consequence chain,
the "but" / "therefore" relationship is already there — name it.

**When this doesn't land:** if the substrate's beats really are sequential — a process
walk-through, a list of independent facts — do not manufacture a reversal that isn't
there. Forced "but"s are as hollow as forced three-part lists.

## Move 1, short declaratives — the attention reset

Retention dips at predictable intervals across a runtime; the viewer's attention is not
a constant. The 1-in-5 short declarative is a micro-reset placed at the sentence level.
Every time the prose lands a 4–6 word sentence that *names* what was just explained —
"Drawing a rectangle is easy", "Hashing is a one-way street" — it renews the viewer's
footing before the next long explanatory sentence. The move's rhythmic job and its
retention job are the same job.

This is why padding with short sentences that don't name anything fails: a reset that
resets to nothing is just a stall.

## Move 6, name everything — the Zeigarnik handle

People remember unfinished tasks markedly better than completed ones; an information gap
triggers a reward-pathway response, and a viewer made to *wait* for a resolution retains
it better than one given no gap. This is the Zeigarnik effect.

A concept that has been *named* but not yet fully explained is an unfinished task — an
open task the brain keeps accessible. "Name everything" gives the viewer a noun to carry
forward, and that noun is a handle on an open loop. When you name "rasterization" early
and pay it off late, the name is doing retention work the entire span between. The move
also enables progressive disclosure: a named concept can be deepened a layer at a time
because the viewer already has the handle.

## Promise moves — the curiosity gap, already in the corpus

`example-passages.md` already shows this. The UUID promise move teases "a crucial third
option" and holds it until late. That tease is a curiosity gap: curiosity comes not from
the unknown itself but from *awareness of the gap* between what the viewer knows and what
they don't. The promise move works because it makes the viewer aware of a specific gap
and implies it will close.

Move 10, the personal-context bridge, is a close relative — it creates the curiosity of
*recognition* ("you've done this — here's why"), which is why the audience responds to it
so strongly.

## Curiosity gaps and open loops — awareness, not instruction

**This section is awareness, not instruction.** Open loops, the curiosity gap, and the
Zeigarnik effect are named here so you can *recognize* them, not so you place them. There
is no "open a loop at 25%, 50%, 75%". There is no required number of loops. The moment
this guidance tells you *where* a loop goes, it has become a structural scaffold —
exactly the failure mode `learnings/script-writer-spec-over-restriction.md` documents — and it is
wrong.

What the phenomena are, so you can recognize them:

- An **open loop** is a gap deliberately revealed and left unresolved — a named concept
  not yet explained, a question posed, a "third option" promised. The brain treats it as
  an active goal and spends resources keeping it accessible until it closes.
- A loop needs both halves: an explicit setup (the gap is revealed) and an implied
  promise (it will close). A gap with no implied resolution is just confusion.

The hard constraint: **a tease lands only where the substrate genuinely has something
worth withholding.** The UUID "third option" and text-rendering's "something strange has
to happen" were not placed by a checklist — the prose arrived at them because the
substrate had a real reveal to hold back. If the topic has nothing to withhold — a
straightforward process, a single counterintuitive fact deepened in place — you write no
loops, and the script is not worse for it. The substrate decides, not this file.

## Where the dips actually bit — two measured failures

Two retention failures from a real long-form video on this channel, named here so you can
recognize the *shape* — not as slots to fill.

**The first ten seconds decide half the audience.** The video lost ~50% of viewers by
0:42. The opening ramped instead of hooking. This is the hook-and-payoff frame failing at
the front: if the first line is a frame-setting sentence rather than the promise itself,
the viewer never receives the front half of the contract and leaves before the body can
pay anything off. The fix is Step 4b's cold open — the first line *is* the hook and the
concrete image lands inside ~10 seconds. Every one of the 237 transcripts in the repo-root
`docs/creator-patterns.md` opens this way; none opens on a greeting, a logo, or "in this
video".

**A subtopic with no reset drags the back half off a cliff.** The same video shed most of
its remaining audience across a ~2.5-minute stretch where one idea ran with no reset move
firing — no short declarative, no "But" pivot, no newly named concept. The reset moves
(Move 1, Move 2, Move 6) are not only sentence-level rhythm; their *absence over a long
span* is where attention leaks. The diagnostic — not a checklist: if you can read a stretch
longer than about a minute and find no short declarative, no pivot, and no new named
handle, the prose has gone slack there, and that is where viewers leave. Reset it with the
moves you already have. `docs/creator-patterns.md` §3 shows how twelve channels keep no
single idea un-reset for more than ~45–60 seconds.

These are *evidence for the moves the other files already define.* They place nothing. If
they tempt you to count seconds or label beats, re-read the section below.

## What this file is not

- Not a new set of moves. Draft from `sentence-rhythm.md`.
- Not a structure. No arc, no beat list, no loop placement, no labeled slots.
- Not a checklist. If you find yourself counting loops or labeling beats in the prose or
  in comments, stop — re-read `learnings/script-writer-spec-over-restriction.md`. That is the
  captcha failure mode returning.
