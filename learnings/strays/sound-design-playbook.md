# Sound & Music Playbook — Long-Form Technical Explainer

## Music Bed Strategy

- **The bed tracks the arc, not the clock.** One continuous bed feels flat over 8-9 min. Switch beds at chapter boundaries (birth → server question → Node.js → terror reign). Each new section gets a track whose energy matches its emotional temperature: curious/sparse for setup, driving/percussive for momentum, dark/tense for the "terror reign," warm/resolved for the verdict + landing.
- **Duck hard under narration.** Bed sits ~12-18 dB below VO during speech (sidechain or manual automation). The bed is felt, not heard, while words land. It only surfaces in the gaps between sentences and at section seams.
- **Drop to silence before the big hit.** The single most powerful move: kill the bed entirely 0.5-1.5s *before* a payoff line or reveal, then slam the impact into the silence. Contrast is the whole trick — "the loudest explosion only works if preceded by near silence."
- **Swell into peaks.** Let the bed rise (lift the duck, add a layer/riser) on the run-up to a peak, then either crest with the moment or cut out for the silence-then-impact play.
- **VO is always king.** If you can't hear a word, the bed is too loud. Target roughly VO at -14 to -16 LUFS integrated, bed riding well under it.

## SFX Taxonomy

| On-screen event | Cue family | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Element appears / pops in | Pop / tick / soft transient | Short, dry, high — lands on the frame it appears |
| Text/label types or counts | Tick / typewriter / UI blip | Tiny, rhythmic, one per beat |
| Number or stat snaps in | Ding / tick / mechanical snap | Crisp, confident, single hit |
| Element leaves / dismisses | Reverse whoosh | Air pulling away, tail outward |
| Scene / chapter transition | Whoosh (layered) | Directional sweep matching the wipe |
| Something breaks / error | Glitch / digital crunch / impact | Distorted, abrupt, slightly ugly |
| Tension build before reveal | Riser | Rising pitch/noise, 1-3s, resolves on the hit |
| Payoff line lands | Boom / sub-drop / BRAAAM | Low, layered (sub + brass + texture), compressed for punch |
| Triumph / celebration | Swell + bright accent | Warm, ascending, room-filling |

Every cue must be **motivated by something on screen or in the words** — sync frame-accurate. An unmotivated SFX reads as amateurish noise.

## Density Rules

- **Motivation gate:** no cue without a visual or rhetorical trigger. Don't fill silence — silence is a tool.
- **Roughly 1 deliberate SFX per 3-6s of content** in active passages; far sparser in reflective ones. If two cues fall within ~300ms and aren't a designed stack, cut one.
- **Reserve the heavy artillery.** Booms/sub-drops/risers are for the 3-5 genuine peaks only. Used everywhere, they stop meaning anything.
- **Silence is structural.** A 1-2s clearing before a payoff is itself a cue. Protect it — no stray whoosh leaking in.
- **Motion principle:** every second has motion; stillness capped at 1-2s. Sound reinforces this — a tick/pop on each entrance keeps the ear moving in lockstep with the eye, so the viewer never registers a lull.

## Do / Don't

- **Do** cut the bed to silence before the verdict and the landing payoff — they're the two earned silences.
- **Do** switch beds at the Node.js and terror-reign boundaries; don't ride one track the whole way.
- **Do** anchor every SFX to a transcript word or a visual frame.
- **Don't** spend a boom/riser on a minor entrance — bankrupt the peaks and the real ones go flat.
- **Don't** let the bed compete with VO; if in doubt, duck harder.
- **Don't** stack two transients within ~300ms unless it's a designed impact stack.
- **Don't** score over a stillness that's doing work — let the gap before "because it was already there" sit.
