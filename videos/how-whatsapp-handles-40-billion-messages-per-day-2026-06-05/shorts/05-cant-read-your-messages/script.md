WhatsApp can't read your messages. Literally can't.

<!-- COUNTER-TWIST -->
Not "won't" — can't. The keys that would unlock them don't exist on WhatsApp's servers at all.

<!-- BODY -->
In twenty sixteen, end-to-end encryption switched on by default for over a billion people, built together with the team behind Signal.

It starts with a handshake called X3DH, which lets two people agree on a shared secret key — even when one of them is fast asleep with their phone off. <!-- TTS: X3DH = "X-three-D-H" --> Then something called the Double Ratchet takes over, and it derives a brand-new key for every single message you send. Steal one key, and you unlock exactly one message. Nothing else.

The server still routes the envelope to the right place. It just never, ever learns what's inside.

But that blindness has a bill. A server that can't read your messages can't search them for you, can't scan them for spam, and can't rebuild your chat history when you switch to a new phone.
