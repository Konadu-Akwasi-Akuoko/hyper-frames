WhatsApp can't read your messages. Literally can't.

Not won't. Can't. The keys that would unlock them don't exist on WhatsApp's servers. At all.

In twenty sixteen, end-to-end encryption switched on by default. For over a billion people. They built it with the team behind Signal.

It starts with a handshake called X-three-D-H. It lets two people agree on a shared secret key. Even when one of them is fast asleep, phone off.

Then the Double Ratchet takes over. It derives a brand-new key for every single message you send. Steal one key, and you unlock exactly one message. Nothing else.

The server still routes the envelope to the right place. It just never, ever learns what's inside.

But that blindness has a bill. A server that can't read your messages can't search them. It can't scan them for spam. And it can't rebuild your chat history when you switch to a new phone.
