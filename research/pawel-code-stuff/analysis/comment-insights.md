# PawelCodeStuff — comment-mining insights

**Sources studied:** 622 comments pulled across all 8 videos via `yt-dlp --write-comments`, sorted by top. Raw data in `comments/raw/`, structured per-video JSON in `comments/clean/`, aggregate CSV at `comments/clean/_all-comments.csv`.

The audience has been telling Pawel exactly what's working and what isn't. This document distills that signal into actionable do's and don'ts for our own channel.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The audience overwhelmingly loves the content — they describe their experience as "ah-ha moments," call him underrated, and pre-commit to coming back when he hits 100K subs. **Two creator comparisons recur**: Fireship ("like Fireship in his prime") and ByteByteGo (animation quality). Both are aspirational positionings.

But there is one consistent, high-volume criticism that should change how we approach our own production:

**52 separate top comments across 7 of 8 videos flag the AI voice. Total: 379 likes on AI-complaint comments.** This is the single most-recurring negative signal in the entire dataset, and it's getting *more* prominent on the bigger videos (20 AI complaints on the text-rendering breakout, including one with 30 likes for an audible pronunciation glitch).

Pawel himself has confirmed in his own reply that he uses ElevenLabs for voiceover, Figma for animation, and DaVinci Resolve for editing — and he openly asked viewers whether he should switch back to his real voice. He didn't. Whether that's the right call for him with his accent is debatable; for *us*, it's straightforward: **use a real voice from day one and we permanently sidestep the channel's only consistent criticism.**

The rest of the do's and don'ts below are second-order — but the AI voice issue alone is worth the entire research pass.

### Pawel's own admission and ask

In a reply on the file-compression video (10 likes), Pawel confirmed:

> "to be honest - yes the voiceover is generated by 11labs because my English pronunciation isn't very good, and I thought using an ai voice that sounds human would be a better approach. but the script part, thumbnail and animations are made by me. He kept the AI voice. The complaints kept coming.

### The two pronunciation glitches that broke the illusion

These two specific moments are referenced repeatedly as "this is what told me it was AI":

- "Two point five-ty eight bits" (mouse-keys video) — 29 likes
- "Lives" pronounced as "lie-vs" instead of "livs" (3-databases video, 5:58) — 6 likes
- "Rows" pronounced as "raus" (3-databases video, 0:40) — 1 like

Modern TTS handles 90% of words perfectly; the 10% that breaks gets noticed. For technical content with unusual numbers, dates, and units, that 10% is a constant landmine.

### What this means for us

**Use a real human voice from day one.** That's the whole lesson. If Pawel switched to his own voice today, he'd lose the AI-voice complaints (~6.5% of all top-comment volume) and probably gain a perceptible bump in like rate. We can have that benefit from the start without a switching cost.

If accent is a concern, the right answer isn't TTS — it's pacing. Slow the read, articulate, post-process audio cleanup. Real voice with a non-native-English accent reads as authentic; AI voice reads as hollow even when technically clearer.

Sorted by frequency and like volume across the corpus.

### 3a. The "ah-ha" moment — by far the most-liked comment type

The single highest-liked comment in the dataset (919 likes, text-rendering video) is just: *"lowkey the best video I've watched in a week."* The second (536 likes) extends an insight: *"That becomes even more impressive once you consider diacritics and kerning…"* The third (502 likes): *"Oh so THAT's why text often has colored edges! Cleared up an age-old mystery for me, thanks!"*

Pattern across all five top-comment lists:
- "Got an answer to a question I never knew" (54 likes, password hashing)
- "I never even knew that password hashing was intentionally slow" (3 likes)
- "12 years of software engineer and i m learning something complety new today" (11 likes, mouse keys)
- "now i know why that there are exploits involving fonts" (220 likes, text rendering)

**The audience loves the moment when a familiar everyday thing reveals an unsuspected depth.** This is the entire reason the script formula works — beat 8 (the "reframe") is what they're describing. Every video should have at least one of these moments. Two is better.

### 3b. "How did you make these animations?" — high-frequency curiosity

This question comes up on almost every video, including from the very first upload. Multiple commenters specifically ask about the visual style. Pawel revealed his stack in a reply (8 likes, password hashing): *"yes it is all manual — figma + davinci resolve."*

For our own channel — given that we have the HyperFrames advantage that he doesn't — we should expect this question even more often, and we should answer it. Either with a pinned comment, an end-card on each video, or eventually a "how I make these" video of our own. Don't be shy about it.

The "underdog about to break out" framing is itself attractive to a portion of the audience — they like being early. We can lean into it for the first 10–20 videos by *not* having the over-produced trappings of an established channel.

## 4. The five recurring criticisms (in priority order)

### Priority 1 — AI voice (covered above)

### Priority 2 — Mobile-screen graphic legibility

> "your graphics appear way too small on a phone screen. Keep it up" — 2 likes, file compression
>
> "Cool video, but maybe increase the contrast of your graphics a bit 😅 I thought my brightness was low but nah I just can't see it" — 33 likes, UUID
>
> "Great video! My only comment is that a lot of the text is too small to see on mobile. Since many of your frames have lots of dark space, you might as well bump up the font size imo." — 2 likes, password hashing
>
> "i can barely see anything in the video its too small" — UUID
>
> "please do be careful about scaling better your diagrams, even on a computer screen, text is barely readable" — UUID

This complaint hits on the bigger videos because more mobile viewers means more people on small screens. **Tactical fix for our work**: any text in a HyperFrames composition that needs to read on mobile should be sized for 360px-wide viewing first, scaled up for desktop second. Use HyperFrames `inspect` to verify contrast ratios and minimum-font-size in actual frame previews.

### Priority 3 — Pacing too fast or too vague on dense moments

> "the DCT segment went too fast for me. I skipped back and watched it again, but it wasn't clear to me which areas of the grid get rounded down to 'zero' — etc. That part of the script was too vague. Sometimes LLMs condense information so much that they go too vague" — 5 likes, file compression
>
> "Honesty good video on a kind of interesting concept. You didn't have to frame it in such a dramatic way but I was plesently supprised at the depth. Very much enjoyed the visuals, but the the script s[uffered]" — 1 like, text rendering

The script formula's strength (rabbit-hole escalation) becomes a weakness when one of the cycles is over-condensed. **Tactical fix**: budget at least 45 seconds per technical concept inside an escalation cycle. If we have to say "this gets reduced to zero," show *which* values, with at least three examples.

### Priority 4 — Frame rate

> "Please consider making your videos at 60 fps! The animations would look so much smoother <3" — 93 likes, text rendering

This was the highest-liked production criticism in the entire dataset. 93 likes for a 60fps request is significant — it means a lot of viewers noticed the animation could look better. **Tactical fix**: render HyperFrames output at 60fps. Whatever the cost in render time, it's clearly the production lever the audience cares about most.

### Priority 5 — Technical accuracy / oversimplification

The expert viewers in the audience push back on simplifications. Examples:

> "You forgot non linear alpha blending/gamma correction. One of the most important components." — 23 likes, text rendering
>
> "1:20 I have some very bad news for that explanation. This is basically just the 3rd normal form (3NF). Also you can't have 1-bit pointers to a table or dictionary." — 2 likes, 3 databases
>
> "Actually if you read DDIA. You would know what's the problem of using auto-increment. In many modern application where you have to scale your database beyond 1 single partition. Having auto-increment becomes a bottleneck." — 3 likes, UUID
>
> "the 'guessable id' is not a real vulnerability if your authentication system is correct" — 12 likes, UUID

These corrections aren't fatal but they cluster on the videos with the strongest authority claims. **Tactical fix**: when we make a strong general claim, immediately add the qualifier. Not "guessable IDs are a security vulnerability" but "guessable IDs are a vulnerability *when paired with weak authorization* — which is more common than you'd think." The qualifier costs 6 words and disarms the corrector.

## 5. Audience composition

Reading between the lines of the comments, we can characterize the audience quite specifically:

- **Senior to mid engineers, not bootcamp newbies.** "12 years software engineering," people referencing Designing Data-Intensive Applications, people who've used VeraCrypt, who reference 3NF, who can spot 11labs voice glitches. This is not a beginner audience.
- **Sophisticated enough to detect AI voice instantly** — and a meaningful subset considers it disqualifying.
- **Will correct technical claims publicly.** Often constructively, sometimes pedantically.
- **Care about authenticity over polish.** The Fireship comparisons come with explicit "don't take the fireship route" warnings — they want him to stay independent and content-pure.
- **Visual people.** The animation question recurs constantly; visual-quality complaints (60fps, contrast, small text) come from people who care about how it looks.
- **They like being early.** "Found this channel early" is a real positive sentiment, not a throwaway.

What this means for content selection: **don't simplify down to a beginner audience.** The depth is what they pay attention for.

| Comment genre | Example | Why it works |

2. **End on a reframe that triggers the "aha" moment**. The 502-like text-rendering comment is reacting to *exactly* the synthesis-and-reframe beat (#7 + #8 in the script analysis).

## 7. Pawel's tech stack — disclosed in his own replies

From his replies on the file-compression and password-hashing videos:

- **Voiceover:** ElevenLabs ("11labs"), AI-generated. Reason given: "my English pronunciation isn't very good."
- **Animation:** Figma (manual)
- **Editing:** DaVinci Resolve

Notably absent: any motion-graphics tool (After Effects, Lottie, etc.). Animations are done as static Figma frames cut together in DaVinci. This explains the *visual style* — high contrast, geometric, no easing curves, no perspective. Everything is essentially a slideshow with very tight cuts.

This is a meaningful gap *we* can exploit. With HyperFrames we have:
- Real animation (transitions, easing, particle systems, shader transitions, GSAP, WAAPI, anime.js)
- Word-level transcript-keyed timing
- Programmatic primitives (pixel grids, Bézier canvases, algorithm visualizers)
- Compositional reuse via blocks
- Real 60fps render (since the audience asked)

If we ship real animation against scripts at his level of writing quality, we have a meaningful production-quality differentiator from day one. The audience has already told us they care about this — *and* that they want 60fps, *and* that they want better contrast and bigger mobile text. Three of the four clean wins are on visual production.

## 10. Pawel's reply patterns — how he handles community

Of 17 uploader replies in the dataset:

- 13 of 17 are short gratitude ("thanks!!!", "thank you brother", "happy to hear that")
- 2 substantive replies (both on file-compression — his AI-voice disclosure and a follow-up version)
- 1 commitment ("there will be separate video about postgres too!")
- 0 replies engaging with technical corrections
- 0 replies engaging with the AI-voice criticism on later videos (only on the early one)

Behavior changed over time:
- File compression: 5 replies (early, lots of personal engagement)
- Password hashing: 8 replies (peak engagement)
- Text rendering: 1 reply (300+ comments, gave up engaging individually)

**Lesson for us**: while comment volume is small, reply substantively to top comments. Pin the best comment per video. Once volume scales, shift to "pinned response" mode rather than per-comment replies. Don't go silent.

One missing move: Pawel doesn't pin any comments in the dataset. Pinning is free signal — it tells the algorithm and the audience what reaction the video was designed to provoke. We should pin the strongest "aha moment" comment on each video.

## 11. Drop-in checklist for our channel

### Production
- [ ] **Real human voice, not TTS.** Even with accent, even slightly slower. Script the read for breath; post-process for clarity.
- [ ] **Render at 60fps.** Highest-liked production complaint (93 likes) was about frame rate.
- [ ] **Mobile-first text sizing.** Preview every composition at 360px wide before shipping. Bump font size if hard to read.
- [ ] **Higher graphic contrast.** Avoid gray-on-dark. Test in HyperFrames preview at low brightness.
- [ ] **One real animated transition per scene.** This is the HyperFrames advantage; use it.

### Script
- [ ] **At least one "personal context" bridge per video.** Connect to something the viewer has done themselves ("you've moved your mouse to generate a key in VeraCrypt; that's why").
- [ ] **At least one synthesis/reframe beat that triggers the "aha" moment.** Beat 7+8 in the script-pattern analysis.
- [ ] **Add qualifiers proactively to strong claims.** "This is a vulnerability *when X*" not "this is a vulnerability."
- [ ] **45+ seconds per technical concept inside an escalation cycle.** Don't condense the dense moments.
- [ ] **Don't simplify for beginners.** The audience is mid-to-senior engineers. Match their level.

### Community
- [ ] **Pin the best "aha moment" comment** on each video.
- [ ] **Reply substantively to top 5 comments** per video for the first 20 videos.
- [ ] **Answer the "how did you make these animations?" question** — pinned comment, end card, or eventual standalone video.
- [ ] **Acknowledge corrections** explicitly when they land. Don't dodge expert pushback.
- [ ] **Don't mention sponsorships, ads, or commercial partnerships** in the first 20 videos. Audience explicitly fears the "fireship route."

### Channel positioning
- [ ] **Lean into "early discovery" framing.** Bare-minimum chrome, no over-produced intros, no "subscribe for more" sequences. Let the underdog appeal compound.
- [ ] **Sit between Fireship (energy) and ByteByteGo (depth).** Our wedge: HyperFrames-quality animation paired with deep-dive scripts.
- [ ] **Don't copy Pawel's title or thumbnail formulas.** They're now him. Our visual identity needs to be its own — see the thumbnail-patterns analysis.
